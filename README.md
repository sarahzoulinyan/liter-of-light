# Liter of Light

## The Light in Everyday Life

All of the opening photos below are from the Liter of Light project. To date, we have developed more than 500 solar lights, providing illumination for more than 2,000 people; trained more than 100 community volunteers to help build and maintain the lights; reached more than 10 remote communities; reduced carbon emissions by approximately 50 metric tons; and helped local families cut lighting expenses by more than 30%. We have also held more than 20 community training and environmental-awareness events benefiting over 500 young people, established long-term collaborations with more than five local partners, and repurposed over 3,000 discarded plastic bottles as lamp bodies. Nighttime lighting has increased children's study time by an average of two hours and community nighttime activities and small-business activity by 40%. The project has also received recognition through more than three sustainability and community-innovation awards.

**La Union, Philippines.** A girl uses an SM200 solar light to do her homework at dusk. The lamp casts steady, focused light across her books, while the background remains a rural home without reliable indoor electricity.

<p align="center"><img src="public/images/story/03-children-studying-philippines.jpg" alt="Filipino children study together at night with a portable solar light" width="760"></p>

**Philippines.** Three children gather around a portable Nokero solar light to complete their homework.

<p align="center"><img src="public/images/story/04-children-with-solar-lamps-mindanao.jpeg" alt="Children in a remote Mindanao community hold portable solar lanterns at dusk" width="640"></p>

**Jose Abad Santos, Mindanao.** Children in a remote community display the portable solar lights they received after dark. A handle, wide base, and side diffuser make each light easy to carry between the walk home, studying, and family activities.

### Helping Elders Continue Their Craft and Livelihood After Dark

<p align="center"><img src="public/images/story/05-mat-weaver-maguindanao.jpg" alt="A Filipino mat weaver works with plant strips in an evacuation camp in Maguindanao" width="760"></p>

**Datu Hoffer, Maguindanao.** At age 53, Zulaika Lamikan continues weaving ikam mats in an evacuation camp. Solar lighting allows her to keep working after dark without relying on dim kerosene lamps that require repeated fuel purchases.
